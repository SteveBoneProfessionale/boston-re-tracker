Hello Ms. Ashman and Mr. Bruggemann,

I'm building a commercial real estate development tracker covering several Rhode
Island municipalities, including Cranston. It aggregates publicly filed planning
and development materials — primarily agendas and minutes from the Secretary of
State's Open Meetings Portal — so that development activity can be viewed by
municipality and by area within a municipality.

I'm writing about one specific data question.

For the "projects by area" view, I need neighborhood boundaries. Providence and
Pawtucket both publish authoritative neighborhood layers through their own GIS
services, so those were straightforward. For Cranston I have not been able to
identify an official published layer, and I would rather ask than guess.

Two specific questions:

1. **Does the City of Cranston publish an official neighborhood or village
   boundary layer** — as a GIS service, shapefile, or map — that you would
   consider authoritative for describing where in the city a project is located?

2. If not, **is there a geography the Planning Department would consider the
   appropriate substitute** for describing project locations — for example
   zoning district, ward, or census tract?

For context on what I found: the statewide RIGIS "Villages" layer contains only
five villages within Cranston's boundary, which is clearly not a usable
description of the city. I also located an ArcGIS layer titled "Neighborhoods
Redistricting 2020" containing 28 neighborhood names — Arlington, Auburn,
Edgewood, Eden Park, Garden City, Knightsville, Meshanticut, Oaklawn/Brookfield,
Pawtuxet, Stone Hill, Thornton, Western Cranston and others. The names look
correct to me, but the layer is published under an individual account rather
than a City one, so I have no way to confirm whether it reflects boundaries the
City actually recognizes, or whether it was assembled for the 2020 redistricting
process specifically.

If that layer is in fact the City's work and is appropriate to use, knowing that
would be very helpful. If it isn't, I would rather fall back to zoning district —
which comes directly off your own agendas — than display neighborhood names the
City doesn't stand behind.

I'm not requesting any non-public records, and there's no urgency. A short reply
either way would save me from publishing something inaccurate.

Thank you for your time.
